# NewWave Africa — four-minute capstone video

NW-31 · Narration and filming plan, not a recorded video. Read at a natural pace and rehearse to fit 3–5 minutes. Show only verified behavior from the exact filmed release.

## Narration

### 0:00–0:40 — problem and beneficiary

An aspiring storyteller may have an idea worth sharing but limited access to a writing mentor, recording equipment or publishing opportunities. Consider a student who can use a school or library computer, but needs help turning an idea into a structured story.

NewWave Africa explores an education-focused solution: practical tools that help emerging creators develop their own work, alongside ways for readers to explore varied perspectives. We will test this need locally; we do not assume every African community faces the same barriers.

### 0:40–2:05 — one creator journey

In Creator Studio, our student chooses the Story and writing canvas. Prompts help them think through characters, setting, narrative and attribution. They decide the words; the tool helps organise their thinking.

They can download an editable planning brief, write a fictional story, choose its metadata and preview it. Before saving, they confirm authorship or permission. The draft can then be retrieved in this browser, including after a reload.

Saving here does not publish the story. Shared browsers need care because other users may see saved drafts. For this demonstration, we use invented content.

Recording is another way to capture ideas. Optional English transcription sends selected audio to Deepgram only after consent and with a session credential. The creator reviews the transcript before adding it. Optional narration can turn up to five thousand characters of written text into a downloadable WAV using one of thirty-six English voices. Provider access and quota are required; sponsored voice usage is a future step.

Readers can search fictional samples, choose interests and adjust diversity weighting. Each story's explanation describes the local ranking rules. This illustrates variety; it does not prove fair outcomes or real audience reach.

### 2:05–3:05 — Codex's role and reuse

Codex supported the workflow across planning, implementation, review and documentation. Here is the repository's AGENTS.md operating agreement, its task plan and reusable skills. This feature's pull request shows the change, review evidence and documentation together.

The application uses Next.js, React and TypeScript. Its README explains local setup, and its architecture separates the static GitHub Pages experience from a prepared server edition. Another developer can inspect and extend these components. Show the checks actually run for the selected revision: a passing build does not establish every microphone, device or user outcome.

### 3:05–4:00 — benefits, evidence and next month

The expected benefit is accessible creative practice: support for structuring ideas, drafting and experimenting with voice without specialist production software. A school, library or community organisation could help learners access the tools. That partnership is proposed; it is not operating today.

With another month, we would run the planned ten-reader and ten-creator study, test target devices and shared-browser privacy, and fix the barriers participants identify. Our existing creator threshold is at least eight out of ten completing the core drafting, rights-confirmation and retrieval tasks without help. We would report failures as well as successes.

Then we would reduce data and access costs, explore sponsored voice usage and locally reviewed language support, and prepare controlled publishing with consent and moderation. Accounts and public publishing remain future work.

NewWave Africa starts with a concrete goal: helping emerging storytellers develop their own work, on their own terms.
