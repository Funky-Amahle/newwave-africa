Across Africa, a story can begin in a school garden, a market stall, a recording booth, or a family conversation. What happens next often depends on whether someone can find it, understand its context, and hear the person behind it. NewWave Africa explores that connection between human creativity and thoughtful discovery. Our starting point is simple: many voices should lead to new perspectives.

This is an early storytelling prototype built for the OpenAI and Andela Telling African Stories challenge. It brings discovery and creative planning into one browser-based experience. It is not yet a public publishing platform. Instead, it makes a focused promise: help an audience explore beyond the familiar, while giving creators practical space to develop work they continue to own.

The new homepage introduces that idea through an editorial spotlight. A visitor can move to the next perspective, open a story, or follow a curiosity into the collection. These actions keep the audience in control. There is no automatic carousel deciding what must be watched next. The design invites attention, then lets the reader choose where to go.

The collection contains eight clearly labelled fictional sample stories. They are demonstration material, not real reporting or creator submissions. They help us explore themes including technology, enterprise, heritage, climate, education, the arts, sport, and community. We use them to demonstrate how the product works, without suggesting that a small collection represents the full diversity of a continent.

Search narrows the collection. Choosing an interest changes the order of stories, rather than hiding every other category. The variety control can give less-represented categories more visibility. These are transparent local rules, not a trained artificial intelligence recommender. The value of this prototype is that the audience can see the choice and explore its effect, instead of encountering an unexplained ranking.

When something catches your attention, open the story reader. The story, its category, and its attribution remain together. Save it to a reading list and return to it later in the same browser. That reading list is a private convenience, not an account or a social profile. It does not create a public audience, and it does not send a bookmark to a publishing service.

Listening is another part of the experience, but it must be honest. The current app plays only reviewed recordings that have been explicitly published for a story. If a recording is missing, narration stays unavailable. The app does not substitute a robotic browser voice. We have generated and reviewed a separate Deepgram sample; that is evidence for the sample, not proof that every story already has published audio.

For creators, the studio begins before publication. Five planning canvases support writing, film and animation, journalism, music and audio, and interactive stories. A creator can shape an idea, consider its audience, record permissions and cultural context, and download an editable production brief. The prompts organise creative decisions. They do not replace the author or generate the work on the author's behalf.

The story draft form adds a live preview and browser-local saving. A rights confirmation keeps authorship and permission visible. The microphone tool can capture a local voice recording, subject to browser permission. Recordings are not automatically uploaded or attached to public stories. That boundary matters: a person experimenting with a draft should not accidentally publish their words or their voice.

Discovery insights compare category coverage within this demonstration collection. They do not measure real-world fairness, reach, income, or audience impact. Those outcomes would require a larger evidence base and meaningful evaluation with creators. The prototype makes its limits visible because a product concerned with representation must also be careful about the claims it makes.

The next chapter is a collaboration with real storytellers: test accessibility, language needs, low-bandwidth use, pronunciation, consent, and discovery quality in context. A future publishing service will need protected infrastructure and creator-controlled choices. We want to earn those capabilities through feedback and evidence, rather than imply that they are already complete.

NewWave Africa is an invitation to discover more thoughtfully and create with greater agency. Read a new perspective. Save a story that stays with you. Give an idea a practical beginning. As the technology develops, the principle remains the same: the creator's context, permission, and voice should travel with the work. Many voices. New perspectives. NewWave Africa.
